ZoomInfo for DTC: Audience Intelligence Built for D2C

ZoomInfo solves a B2B problem and doesn't translate to DTC. mymodel is the ZoomInfo for DTC, a consumer-side database of verified, consented behavioral profiles, designed for D2C audience search, segmentation, and outbound after the cookie and iOS resets.

The DTC stack has a missing layer

DTC operators have a Shopify-and-Klaviyo back end, a Meta-and-TikTok acquisition front end, and a thin first-party CRM in the middle. None of that gives them what a B2B team gets from ZoomInfo: a clean, queryable database of the people they want to reach, with enough behavioral context to segment intelligently before spending media against them.

Historically, the substitute has been third-party data providers feeding lookalike models inside ad platforms. That layer is collapsing. Cookies are deprecated, iOS attribution is gone, the data brokers are losing inputs, and lookalikes built on shrinking signal are degrading month over month. CAC is rising on every channel because the targeting layer underneath is getting noisier.

The DTC equivalent of ZoomInfo isn't a different ad platform. It's a database, a real one, with consented, verified, structurally durable signal underneath.

What "ZoomInfo for DTC" needs to be

For DTC operators, the equivalent has to deliver three things:

  • A search-and-segment workflow. Filter consumers by behavior, category interest, household composition, lifecycle stage, and intent, the way B2B teams filter by company, title, and tech stack.
  • Activation, not just intelligence. Identifying the audience is half the job. Reaching them efficiently is the rest. The DTC equivalent has to support direct activation, not just enrichment.
  • Privacy durability. The replacement for cookie-based targeting can't be another inferred-signal scheme. It has to be consented at the unit-record level so it survives the next regulatory tightening.

mymodel was built to be exactly that layer.

How DTC brands actually use mymodel

The motion is recognizable. Define the segment. Build the offer. Deploy. Measure.

  • Define. Filter the consumer database by category usage, brand affinity, purchase frequency, household type, and lifecycle stage. Save as a recurring segment.
  • Offer. Build an Audience Order: a launch promotion, a sample drop, a trial enrollment, a post-purchase research recruit, a winback. Set the per-acceptance budget.
  • Deploy. The order goes to the segment. Users decide whether to accept; acceptance is the consent and the activation event.
  • Measure. Acceptance rate, downstream conversion, repeat behavior, all attributable to identified users without depending on cookies or device IDs.

DTC's targeting problem isn't that ad platforms got worse. It's that the data underneath them got worse, and the architecture was always going to break under privacy law and platform policy. The solve is a consumer database that doesn't depend on either.
