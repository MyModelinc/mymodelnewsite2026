Apollo for DTC: Outbound Reach for D2C Brands

Apollo gave B2B teams a search-and-send motion that compresses the time between identifying a prospect and reaching them. DTC has never had the equivalent, for good reasons. mymodel is the Apollo for DTC: paid, consented outbound to verified consumer behavioral segments, structurally compatible with privacy law and platform policy.

Why DTC has been stuck without an outbound layer

The B2B Apollo motion works because the regulatory and platform layer permits cold outreach in a work context. Pointed at consumers, that motion fails on three sides at once: the law restricts it (CCPA, CPRA, GDPR, TCPA), the platforms enforce against it, and the conversion math collapses because recipients never asked to be reached.

That's why DTC has effectively been a paid-acquisition-only category. Meta, TikTok, Google, influencer, retail media, post-purchase. Every channel runs on inferred targeting layered on shrinking data, and CAC has been moving the wrong way for years. There has been no clean outbound motion DTC operators could run themselves.

What an Apollo for DTC has to look like

The architecture has to invert. Outbound only happens when the recipient has chosen to receive paid offers. Outbound only converts when the recipient is paid for the engagement. Consent is the entry condition; payment is the activation. That makes the motion compliant by design and high-signal by construction.

mymodel runs that motion. Real consumers build a 360° behavioral Stats Card they control. Brands search the audience the same way an Apollo user filters a B2B database, by behavior, category interest, household, lifecycle, intent. Audience Orders deploy into matched segments. Recipients decide whether to accept; acceptance is the consent and the conversion event.

How DTC operators run it

  • Define the segment. Filter by category usage, brand affinity, household type, lifecycle stage, intent. Save and reuse.
  • Build the order. Launch promo, sample drop, subscription trial, winback, post-purchase research. Set the per-acceptance budget.
  • Deploy. The order reaches the segment. Users decide whether to accept. Acceptance is the consent event and the activation.
  • Measure and iterate. Acceptance rate, downstream conversion, repeat behavior, all attributable to identified users without depending on cookies, MAIDs, or pixel-based attribution.

DTC has needed an outbound activation layer for ten years. The reason it didn't exist is that the only architecture that works on the consumer side has to pay the recipient. Once that economic flip happens, the entire motion gets unblocked.
