ZoomInfo for Consumer: The Consent-Based Equivalent

ZoomInfo built a category by aggregating B2B contacts at scale. There has never been a clean equivalent on the consumer side, until now. mymodel is the ZoomInfo for consumer: verified, behaviorally rich profiles that real people build and control, with brand access mediated by paid consent.

Why "ZoomInfo for consumer" has been an unsolved category

ZoomInfo and the wider B2B prospecting stack work because their target, businesses and the people who work at them, leave a public trail. Job titles, company emails, firmographics, hiring signals. The data is messy, but it's there to be aggregated.

Consumers don't work that way. Their behavior, preferences, household context, and buying intent live behind a wall of platforms and inferred signals. The closest the industry has come is third-party data brokers, Acxiom, Experian, the data-broker layer behind ad networks, and that layer is degrading fast. Cookies are deprecated. iOS killed mobile IDs. Privacy law is closing the gaps.

The result: every consumer-facing brand wants something like ZoomInfo for their market, and there has been no clean way to build it. You can't scrape consumers the way you scrape LinkedIn.

The consumer equivalent has to be built differently

The only way to build a real, durable database of consumer audience intelligence is to invert the architecture. Instead of harvesting data through consent nobody reads, you have to make it worth the consumer's time to participate, and pay them when their data is used.

That's what mymodel is. A platform where consumers build a 360° behavioral Stats Card they fully control. They keep it accurate because they get paid every time a brand reaches them through it. The brand-side experience is recognizable to anyone who has used a B2B intelligence tool, search, filter, segment, deploy, but the data underneath is fundamentally different. It's verified by financial commitment from the consumer.

What brands actually get

Brands operating on mymodel run the same kind of motion they would in a B2B intelligence tool, but pointed at the consumer market:

  • Audience search. Filter by behavior, category interest, household composition, prior purchase signals, and dozens of other dimensions consumers verified themselves.
  • Audience Orders. Deploy promotions, product launches, surveys, or research recruits into matched segments. Every recipient is paid for accepting the order.
  • Consent at the transaction level. Not a buried checkbox, a per-offer agreement from a real, identified person.
  • Compliant by construction. The architecture pre-clears the legal questions that the extractive stack is now losing.

The reason there hasn't been a ZoomInfo for consumer is that consumer data couldn't be built the same way. Once you stop trying to harvest it and start paying for it, the database becomes possible, and the signal quality is higher than anything the extractive stack ever produced.
